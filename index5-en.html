📋 What, Where, When to See in Tbilisi

🔹 Tbilisi – The Capital of Georgia

 

Tbilisi is, of course, the capital. But calling it just "the capital" is like calling khinkali "dumplings." Formally similar, but in reality utter nonsense. This is a city where every crack in the asphalt knows something we don't even suspect. Where antiquity embraces modernity so tightly that you can no longer tell who is older. And the nature around it — well, it's like a backdrop for a movie where the hero is bound to fall in love.

 

Tbilisi welcomes you not as a strict guide, but as that Georgian uncle who will first pour you wine and then ask your name. Here, time flows by its own rules: you plan to see one building, and you wake up three hours later in a basement with khachapuri and a new friend named Gia. Ancient fortresses recall kings and caravans, squares hum with street music (sometimes even in harmony), and the balconies of old houses hang over you as if about to share a few centuries' worth of gossip.

And yes, East and West met here and, it seems, became friends forever. You see it in the carvings on doors, the smell of spices at the market, and how an Orthodox church, a mosque, and a synagogue stand side by side — and no one argues.

And the nature around Tbilisi is a whole other story. The city doesn't just stand on hills — it lies on them, breathes, and sometimes even slides — but holds up well. The Kura River winds through the center like a snake that forgot where it was going. Thermal springs warm the earth (and your feet), the climate spoils you almost all year round, and the views from the hills are so stunning that even Instagram swallows enviously. In short, Tbilisi doesn't just rest — it recharges you. And it does so with such generous humor that leaving here sad is simply impossible.

🔹 "The Impregnable Fortress" (Narikala Fortress)

Narikala Fortress – GPS coordinates: 41.6882188, 44.8071236.

View on Google Maps


Somewhere far below, the city hums — echoes of car horns and laughter drift up. You have a choice: climb the winding rocky path on foot, feeling every stone and thistle under your soles, or cheat a little and take the cable car for just 2 lari. I recommend the cable car — if only for the thrill of gliding over the tiled roofs.
But once you step onto the ancient ground of Narikala, time seems to coil into a ring. This fortress is over 1,500 years old. There are no paved paths here — only wild grass and half-ruined walls that have seen the rise and fall of great empires. You find a spot at the edge, catch your breath, and look down. Tbilisi sprawls at your feet like a living mosaic: narrow alleys, whimsical balconies, golden domes of churches, and the mirror-like ribbon of the Kura River. The wind whistles through the ancient stones, and for a moment it seems that a mythical guardian might rise from the ruins. This place is wild, ancient, and incredibly beautiful.

🔹 The White Giant (Sameba Cathedral — Holy Trinity Cathedral)

Sameba Cathedral – GPS coordinates: 41.697498, 44.816666.

View on Google Maps


It is impossible to miss. Wherever you are in Tbilisi, this massive white silhouette rises above the city like a sleeping giant on a hill. But as you approach, the giant seems to come alive. Sameba Cathedral, standing 101 meters tall, is the highest church in Georgia; it doesn't just stand — it soars majestically skyward.
Inside, the city noise fades away. The air is cool and thick with the scent of incense. Your eyes adjust to the semi-darkness, and an amazing picture unfolds: intricate carvings, gilded icons, and frescoes that seem to tell their stories without words. At the altar rests a huge handwritten Bible — so imposing and detailed that next to it, you can't help but feel small, and that feeling brings a wonderful sense of peace. You don't have to be religious to feel something here. This place is filled with tranquility, pride, and quiet reverence.

🔹 A Breath of Fresh Air (Lisi Lake)

View on Google Maps


After long walks on the cobblestone streets, your feet long for something soft. Lisi Lake is the perfect solution. This place, located just a short distance from the center, is a true oasis where Tbilisi can catch its breath. Here, you can rent a pedal boat and glide leisurely across the water, watching carefree ducks.
On the shore, families picnic on the grass, children race go-karts, and active travelers try their hand at rope courses among the treetops in summer. You sit on a bench in the shade, close your eyes, and let the silence envelop you. No car horns, no noisy crowds — only the rustling of leaves and the soft lapping of water. This isn't a museum or a temple, but a pure, simple corner of nature that feels like a true gift.

🔹 "A Village in the City" (Ethnographic Museum)

Address: Tbilisi, Turtle Lake Avenue 1 (near Vake Park and Turtle Lake).
How to get there: Convenient by taxi from the Saburtalo or Vake districts, or on foot / by transport from Vake Park.

View on Google Maps


This is an unusual museum: no glass display cases, no velvet rope barriers. It is a true fairy-tale world under the open sky, nestled in Vake Park near Turtle Lake. You wander through a forest of miniature villages, each brought here from a different corner of Georgia. One resembles a mountain shepherd's hut, another a winemaker's house from the eastern regions.
Peering through old windows and touching rough wooden tools, you vividly imagine how, a hundred years ago, grandmothers baked bread in these very rooms. This is not just history — it's a time machine. And because the trees rustle and birds sing all around, visiting the museum feels less like a boring lesson and more like a pleasant picnic in the company of the past.

👗 About History and Culture

🔹 Not That Sydney


A couple excitedly booked cheap flights to Sydney — they imagined the ocean, the opera house, and kangaroos in caps. Mid-flight, they learned with a slight chill that the destination was the tiny Canadian Sydney in Nova Scotia, where the main attraction is a single traffic light and a very confused moose. Instead of surfing, they were met with cozy rain and conversations about maple syrup.


🔹 The Ruins Need to Be Finished Immediately


A tourist in Rome dutifully examined the ancient Roman ruins for a full half hour, frowning and shaking his head. Then he lashed out at the guide with genuine indignation: "Couldn't you have finished building it properly? The tickets were expensive, and this is just a construction site from the first century! Where's the promised renovation?" The guide politely offered to apply some spackle to the column.

🔹 The Terrifying Night Predator


At an Australian campsite, panic erupted in the middle of the night: tourists ran screaming from their tents, insisting that a giant monster was hissing, growling, and grinding its teeth behind the canvas. In the morning, it turned out the ferocious beast was a sleepy koala simply trying to discreetly clear its throat of eucalyptus leaves. The tourists laughed so hard that the kangaroos got scared.

🔹 Invisible View


A couple spent their last savings on a room in the Alps with a "guaranteed view of the snow-capped peaks." For all seven days, their window was adorned with impenetrable milky fog — very romantic. Only on the last day, when the suitcases were already packed, did the fog theatrically lift, and they finally saw the long-awaited view… a brick wall of the neighboring building with a sign reading "View not included in room rate."

🔹The Tip Trick


In Egypt, a tourist was slyly offered a camel ride for just $1. He was overjoyed and climbed onto the hump. The ride was magical, but when it was time to dismount, the handler sweetly informed him: "Dismounting is a separate option. Only $50." The young man sat on the camel for a long time, asking if there was a discount for those willing to stay in the saddle forever.

🔹 A Little Mix-Up


In Thailand, a girl tenderly offered a banana to an elephant, dreaming of the perfect selfie. The elephant, with imperturbable politeness, accepted the treat, deftly grabbing with its trunk not only the fruit but also her stylish sunglasses, and sent everything into its mouth with visible pleasure. After lunch, it proudly walked off into the sunset, leaving the girl in complete darkness and with a newfound love for nature.

🔹 Sea Tan


A guy decided that sunscreen was just marketing and heroically fell asleep in a deck chair on a cruise, covering his chest with a book. He woke up a celebrity — against a crimson, lobster-red body, a perfectly white rectangle in the shape of a book stood out. For the next two days, he was called "Book Tiger" and wore a T-shirt even in the sea.

🔹 Night Visitor


In a cozy Costa Rican hotel, a tourist jumped up in the middle of the night at a soft rustling sound. Her heart pounded — a burglar! In the darkness, she screamed, turned on the light, and froze: a little raccoon with her toothbrush in its teeth stared at her with an expression that said, "This is mine now." It calmly jumped out the window, leaving the woman with insomnia and the eternal question of how often raccoons brush their teeth after dinner.

🔹 Self-Propelled Luggage


A traveler's suitcase got lost during a layover and embarked on its own round-the-world journey. While the exhausted owner rested on a beach, his faithful suitcase managed to visit Dubai, Tokyo, Rio, and, judging by the stickers, even catch a glimpse of the Northern Lights. By the end of the vacation, the suitcase returned covered in stickers and stamps, looking far more experienced than its owner.

📅 Traveler's Notes

🔹 Kldeisi Waterfall, Kvemo Kartli, Georgia

GPS coordinates: 41.6441, 44.3944 (approximate coordinates of the Kldeisi Gorge).

View on Google Maps


Kldeisi Waterfall is located in the Kvemo Kartli region of Georgia, near the village of Mali-Kldeisi (Little Kldeisi) in the Tetritskaro Municipality.

Key information:

Location: Kvemo Kartli region, Tetritskaro Municipality, near the village of Patara-Kldeisi (Little Kldeisi), about 1.5–2 hours by car from Tbilisi, at an elevation of about 1,380 meters above sea level. The waterfall is hidden in a wooded valley. The place is wild, undeveloped, and great for trekking.

 

Features: A picturesque natural spot, especially beautiful in spring when the surrounding landscape is lush with fresh greenery. This route is popular among hiking clubs and outdoor enthusiasts.

 

Difficulty: Moderate; the total hiking distance is typically about 6–7 kilometers.

 

Nearby attractions: Besides the tall waterfall, in the vicinity of the Kldeisi villages you can see medieval churches (e.g., the Church of the Virgin from the 10th–11th centuries) and natural cooling caves (Chorkhebi).

 

How to get there and useful tips:

The road passes through the picturesque historical areas of Trialeti.

 

The best time to visit is spring (especially May): the waterfall is most powerful, and nature is at its brightest and most beautiful.

 

Since the roads in the village itself are unpaved and undeveloped, it is recommended to go with a high-clearance vehicle or as part of an organized trekking tour.

 

The Secret of Kldeisi Waterfall
The road to Kldeisi village feels like a journey into the past. This place in the Kvemo Kartli region, high in the mountains of Tetritskaro Municipality (at 1,380 meters above sea level), greets you with clean air and silence, broken only by the rustling of the wind and the distant roar of water.

My adventure began with the anticipation of encountering natural beauty, but soon I understood. The sun-drenched forest path led me through the picturesque historical landscapes of Trialeti. The roughly 6–7 kilometer (round-trip) moderate route wound through the densely overgrown gorge, as if carefully guarding its treasures.

As I walked, I couldn't shake the feeling that the rocks themselves were watching me. The name "Kldeisi" means "rocky" in Georgian, and it's easy to see why. On both sides, limestone cliffs rose up, forming a natural amphitheater that amplified the growing roar of water. Finally, I turned a corner — and saw it. Kldeisi Waterfall cascaded over ancient stones, creating a magnificent, sun-drenched curtain. The water crashed into a clear pool surrounded by bright green moss. It was a spectacle of pure, primal magic.

"Natural Refrigerator" (Chorkhebi Cave): The greatest wonder of this place, about which local shepherds have told tales for centuries. In the basalt cracks and caves near the waterfall, cold air is trapped so effectively that ice formations do not melt even in the hot summer. Locals used these natural freezers to store dairy products.

 

Medieval Guardian Churches: On the way to the waterfall, you'll find ruins of an ancient fortress, the Church of St. George, and the Church of the Virgin (10th–11th centuries) with ancient inscriptions in the Asomtavruli alphabet. Locals believed these sanctuaries protected the gorge from enemies.

Suddenly, I felt that the waterfall was no longer just a natural wonder for me — it had become a guardian. The cool spray on my face and the thunder of falling water now felt different — as if filled with the breath of history and the spirit of mystery. The way back down the mountain was full of awe: I felt as if I were walking in the footsteps of a legend, and in my heart, the legend of Kldeisi Waterfall continued to echo.
